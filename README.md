# Online Retail RFM Analysis & Customer Segmentation Dashboard

## Project Overview
This project transforms raw transactional data from a UK‑based online retailer into a strategic customer segmentation model. Using **Excel (Power Query, PivotTables, and interactive visualizations)**, I cleaned the data, built an RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) framework, and delivered an executive dashboard with actionable business recommendations.

The goal: help the business understand who their customers are, which ones are at risk of leaving, and where to focus marketing efforts to increase revenue.

---

## Data & Methodology

### Data Source
- **Dataset:** Online Retail Dataset (UCI Machine Learning Repository / Kaggle)  
- **Records:** ~541,000 transactions from 01/12/2010 to 09/12/2011  
- **Features:** InvoiceNo, StockCode, Description, Quantity, InvoiceDate, UnitPrice, CustomerID, Country

### Data Cleaning (Power Query)
The raw data contained several real‑world quality issues that required careful handling. Every decision is documented and business‑justified:

1. **Null CustomerIDs** – Rows with missing `CustomerID` were removed for customer‑level analysis. These represent internal adjustments or anonymous purchases and cannot be linked to a specific customer.
2. **Cancellations (‘C’ invoices)** – Invoices with a leading ‘C’ indicate full order returns. Investigation revealed that all corresponding original invoices also had null `CustomerID` and were already excluded. Therefore, all cancellation invoices were excluded from the RFM model to avoid distorting customer metrics without a matching valid purchase.
3. **Negative Quantities (non‑C invoices)** – Rows with negative quantities and blank descriptions were examined. They uniformly had null `CustomerID`, confirming they are internal stock adjustments, not customer returns. These were excluded.
4. **Data Enrichment** – A `TotalPrice` column was created as `Quantity * UnitPrice`. Date columns (Year, Month, Day) were extracted for time‑based filtering.

### RFM Segmentation
After cleaning, the RFM metrics were built for each unique customer:

- **Recency** – Days since the customer’s last purchase, measured from `2011-12-10` (the day after the latest transaction in the dataset).  
- **Frequency** – Number of distinct invoices (purchase events) per customer.  
- **Monetary** – Total revenue generated by the customer (sum of `TotalPrice`).

Each metric was scored on a 1‑to‑4 scale using percentile ranks (1 = worst, 4 = best). The combined scores were mapped to six customer profiles:

| Segment           | Description |
|-------------------|-------------|
| **Champions**     | Recent, frequent, high spenders |
| **Loyal Customers** | Regular buyers with solid spend |
| **Potential Loyalists** | Recent buyers with room to grow |
| **At Risk**       | Historically valuable customers who haven’t returned recently |
| **Lost**          | Inactive, low spend, low frequency |
| **Other**         | Uncategorized patterns requiring further review |

## Key Insights & Conclusions

1. **The “At Risk” segment is the most valuable—and the most urgent.**  
   Despite having not purchased recently, these customers contribute nearly **66% of total revenue** and have the **highest average spend ($5,094.10)**. They are past high‑value customers who have quietly lapsed.

2. **Champions are underrepresented.**  
   The healthiest segment (Champions) accounts for only **6.28% of revenue**, with an average spend far below At Risk. This indicates the business is failing to retain its best customers and convert them into loyal, repeating champions.

3. **Extreme geographical concentration.**  
   The UK alone drives **60% of revenue**, with EIRE and the Netherlands the next largest. Most other markets are negligible, representing a major risk if the UK market softens.

4. **The “Lost” segment is negligible in value (0.36% of revenue).**  
   This suggests that once a customer fully churns, they are unlikely to return—reinforcing the need to act **before** they enter this group.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, I recommend the following actions, ordered by potential ROI:

1. **Immediate Win‑Back Campaign for At Risk Customers (Priority #1)**  
   - These high‑spenders have lapsed but are not yet lost. Launch a targeted email campaign with a personalised discount or loyalty incentive.  
   - **Goal:** Recover even 10% of their projected value, which could represent hundreds of thousands in revenue.

2. **Strengthen the Champion & Loyal Customer Retention Program**  
   - Introduce a tiered loyalty program or exclusive offers for these segments.  
   - Keep them engaged so they don’t fall into the At Risk pool.

3. **Market Diversification Strategy**  
   - Pilot aggressive marketing in the next‑tier countries (Netherlands, Australia, Germany) to reduce dependence on the UK.  
   - Localise offers and test small campaigns to gauge price sensitivity.

4. **Regular RFM Refresh & Monitoring**  
   - Schedule the RFM model to update monthly. Track the movement of customers between segments, especially the flow from Loyal/Champion → At Risk.  
   - This makes the dashboard a live operational tool, not a one‑off report.
